# Candidates

Ten one-line fortune cookie messages for software engineers, judged against the
judge's brief: (1) a real fortune cookie for an engineer, (2) funny on first
read with a real punchline, (3) sounds like a fortune cookie, (4) fresh —
not a mug/sticker/conference-slide line, (5) tight, funniest word at the end.

## The winner

> **Your code will work on the first try, and that will worry you more than any bug.** (80 chars)

A fortune cookie is supposed to deliver good news — and this one does, then
turns it into the engineer's worst dread in the same breath. Every engineer
knows it: code that works on the first try is not a relief, it is a mystery,
because you do not know *why* it works and therefore when it will stop.
Lands on first read, specific to the engineer's world, not a stock line, and
tight with the funniest word (*bug*) at the very end. The inversion — a good
fortune that is actually bad news — is what makes it fresh against a field
full of "deploy on Friday" and "works on my machine" jokes.

## The nine it beat

1. **You will write the perfect fix, then realize you fixed the wrong bug.** (71)
   Strong runner-up: classic fortune opening, punchline "wrong bug" at the end.
   Loses to the winner on freshness — "fixed the wrong bug" is a known trope —
   and its twist is smaller (a mistake, not a reversal of fortune itself).

2. **You will be promoted for fixing the bug you introduced last quarter.** (72)
   Good career-disguised-as-bug twist, lands fast. Loses on the ending: the
   funniest word is "introduced" in the middle, "last quarter" lands soft.

3. **The code you wrote at 2 a.m. will become the system everyone is afraid to touch.** (86)
   Vivid and true. Loses on tightness and the ending: the funniest word is
   "2 a.m." in the middle, and "touch" is not the punchline the line needs.

4. **You will find the bug in the line you skipped because it looked too simple to fail.** (82)
   Relatable, fortune voice, good ending ("fail"). Loses on freshness — the
   "obvious line was the bug" observation is well worn — and it is wordier
   than the winner for a smaller laugh.

5. **You will spend four hours debugging, then change one character.** (60)
   The tightest line here and a real laugh. Loses on freshness: the
   one-character fix is a mug-level classic, exactly what criterion 4 punishes.

6. **A junior will fix in five minutes the bug you chased all week.** (63)
   Funny and humbling. Loses on the ending: the funniest words ("five minutes")
   sit in the middle and "week" closes weakly; also reads more like an insult
   than a fortune.

7. **Your standup update will be "almost done" for the third week in a row.** (76)
   Sharp and specific to standups. Loses because it is a complaint about a
   process, not a fortune — it predicts embarrassment, not fate — and the
   punchline ("in a row") is flat.

8. **You will deploy on Friday afternoon, and production will keep the receipt.** (74)
   The Friday-deploy trope with a fresh second half. Loses on freshness:
   "never deploy on Friday" is the single most repeated engineer joke there is,
   and the judge has read it a thousand times.

9. **Your manager will ask for an estimate, then ignore it and set the deadline anyway.** (83)
   True and bitter. Loses because it is a gripe about management, not a fortune
   cookie for an engineer, and it ends on "anyway" — the weakest word in the line.
